A focus workspace that runs on your own machine.

LockinRa keeps your tasks, notes, calendar, focus sessions and screen-time history in one desktop app, with a local AI model that reads them and helps you plan the day, catch yourself drifting, and see where the hours actually went.

// FAQ

The honest answers.

Is my data really private?

Yes. There are no accounts, no servers, and no telemetry. Your tasks, notes, and screen activity stay on your machine, encrypted at rest in the OS keychain. The AI models run locally — nothing is transmitted for us to even receive.

Does it need an internet connection?

No. Once the local models are installed, focus tracking, capture, coaching, and the copilot all work fully offline. Internet is only used if you opt into cloud integrations like Google Calendar or an optional API fallback.

Can it block distracting websites and apps?

Yes. LockinRa includes a built-in site blocker with strict and mild modes, plus the AI focus guardian that classifies each site and app against your current task and nudges you back when you drift. You can block distracting sites straight from your history in one click.

Which AI models does it use?

Local LLMs through Ollama — a curated catalog of 10+ models including Llama, Qwen, Gemma, Phi, Mistral and GLM — plus Whisper for voice. You can also bring your own Ollama model. Switch models, see RAM recommendations and accuracy stats, and install or delete them from inside the app. No terminal required.

Is there a Mac or Linux version?

Yes — all three ship from the same codebase at v1.8.0. Windows comes as an installer, an .msi for managed deployment, and on the Microsoft Store. macOS has a .dmg for Apple silicon and Intel — not notarized yet, so Gatekeeper blocks the first launch. The terminal install sidesteps it entirely; otherwise it is one command, or one trip to System Settings (macOS 15 removed the old right-click → Open shortcut). Linux has an AppImage, a .deb and an .rpm; everything works except desktop-app distraction detection under Wayland, where no protocol exists for reading window titles (the browser extension still covers the web, and X11 is unaffected). Every file, with checksums and the honest per-platform limits, is on the downloads page. An Android companion and web dashboard are further out.

What are the system requirements?

Windows 10/11 (64-bit), macOS 11 Big Sur or newer on Apple silicon or Intel, or Linux x86-64 with glibc 2.35+ (Ubuntu 22.04, Fedora 36 and newer). 8GB RAM for small models, 16GB recommended for the larger ones. A discrete GPU helps inference but isn't required — LockinRa picks a model that fits your hardware and tells you the trade-offs up front.

Will it slow my computer down?

No. The always-on part is a lightweight rule engine — under 1% CPU. The LLM only wakes for genuinely ambiguous calls, and models release their RAM after you go idle. Monitoring steps down when you're away from the keyboard, so an open LockinRa doesn't tax a laptop on battery.

Do I need a GPU?

No. Every model in the catalog runs on CPU; a GPU just makes responses snappier. On setup, LockinRa reads your hardware, recommends a model that fits your RAM, and shows the speed/accuracy trade-offs before you commit.

Which browsers and apps does it work with?

The browser extension covers Chrome and Chromium-based browsers like Edge and Brave (Firefox isn't supported yet). Outside the browser, the desktop app watches the active window directly — VS Code, games, design tools, anything — no plugin needed.

How accurate is the distraction detection?

Around 90% in practice. Classification is a three-stage cascade: instant hard rules, then semantic matching against your current task, and the local LLM only for the genuinely ambiguous. When it does misfire, one click on "Wrong guess" corrects it — and it learns your patterns from there.

How it decides

Four stages. The expensive one runs last.

Every classification starts cheap and stops as soon as it's sure. Most never reach the model at all.

  1. Stage 1

    Hard rules

    A domain and app list. Instant, deterministic, no model involved.

  2. Stage 2

    Your corrections

    The times you've said “no, that was work.” Nearest-match vote, weighted toward recent ones, and it only counts if the matches agree.

  3. Stage 3

    Title keywords

    A cheap read of the page or window title.

  4. Stage 4

    The local model

    Reached only when the first three can't resolve it. This is the one that costs you power, and it's the one that runs least.

  5. Fallback

    No match

    It stays quiet. An app that guesses out loud is worse than one that says nothing.

What leaves

Nothing leaves. Not by policy — by architecture.

There is no account, no sync, and no server to send anything to. You can prove it in about four seconds: pull your network cable and keep working.

  • Stays on your disk

    Tasks, notes, habits, activity, embeddings — a SQLite file on your own machine.

  • Encrypted

    Activity URLs and window titles, semantic memory, integration tokens and vaulted notes — with a key derived from your OS keychain (DPAPI on Windows).

  • Leaves, only if you connect it

    Google, Outlook, GitHub, Slack. Off by default, and off until you sign in yourself.

What isn't encrypted: your task titles and task notes.

They're stored as plain text in that SQLite file. Anyone with your unlocked machine and a SQLite viewer can read them — the same as your notes app, your calendar, and most of your disk. We could have said “encrypted at rest” and let you assume it covered everything. This page said that until August 2026. It was wrong, and it's fixed.

Around the guardian

It guards a workspace, not a timer.

    Tasks, projects, an outliner with backlinks and block references.

  • Capture it

    Voice to text, quick capture, and notes that feed the model's memory.

  • See it honestly

    Screen time by app and site, habits, streaks, and a daily recap.

  • Be argued with

    Three coach voices — supportive, dark humour, drill sergeant. Three ways of refusing the same 2pm deal.

The questions you're about to ask

Does it work offline?

Yes. Pull the cable and keep going — classification and coaching run on your machine. Nothing about the core product needs a network.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign-up, no email, and no licence key. You download it and it runs.

Which model does it use?

Whichever you pick from a catalog of 24, or one you bring yourself. The default is a small one, and nothing downloads until you choose.

Can I just switch it off when it nags me?

You can quit it. But blocks are extend-only while a session runs, and a watchdog puts back what you remove — because the whole product assumes the 2pm you will try to undo the 9am you.

Is there a Mac build?

Not yet. Windows is the primary platform and Linux is in beta at v1.3.0.
